## Medium Post

I recently finished a portfolio research project that connects financial economics concepts with Python implementation.

The workflow covers:

- Market data loading with `yfinance`
- Daily and monthly return analysis
- CAPM-style beta and benchmark correlation versus SPY
- Equal-weight, minimum-volatility, and maximum-Sharpe portfolio construction
- Fixed-weight backtesting and business interpretation

One key takeaway was how different portfolio objectives create different tradeoffs. The maximum-Sharpe portfolio performed best historically, but it was more concentrated. The minimum-volatility portfolio reduced drawdown risk, while equal-weight remained simple and diversified.

This is a portfolio research project, not investment advice or a production trading system.

## Technical Post

I built a Python-based Portfolio Optimization and Factor Research Lab as part of my finance and analytics portfolio.

Project components:

- `pandas` and `NumPy` for return analytics
- `yfinance` for public market data
- CAPM-style beta, alpha, benchmark correlation, tracking error, and rolling beta
- Long-only equal-weight, minimum-volatility, and maximum-Sharpe portfolios
- Fixed-weight historical backtests versus SPY
- Plotly-based figures and markdown reports for business interpretation

The project intentionally keeps the methodology transparent. It also documents limitations: no transaction costs, no walk-forward validation, no live trading, and no investment recommendation. The goal is to show a reproducible finance analytics workflow that is useful for early-career investment analytics, financial data analytics, and corporate finance roles.
